Why it works this way

The awkward questions.

For artists: the questions people ask first, answered plainly, including where a choice is a trade rather than a win.

Listening, not making music? How paying works is the page for you.

What happens if someone doesn't pay?

Your paid tracks only play for listeners whose app holds a wallet with money in it, and the app pays for every minute automatically. Anyone else hears just the tracks you've made free.

Your editor's Earnings tab shows each listener next to what they've paid. If someone isn't paying, tap Block and your paid tracks stop playing for them within half an hour.

The honest limit: a determined person can make a new wallet, or build their own app to skip paying until you notice. Treat it as a deterrent, not a lock. Most people simply pay.

Why digital dollars, and not PayPal or Stripe?

Because of what a tiny payment costs. A card payment has a fixed fee of 20 to 30 cents, which would swallow a four-cent payment whole. A transfer of digital dollars (USDC, on the Solana network) costs a fraction of a cent, which is what makes paying by the minute possible.

It also means no company sits in the middle holding your money, so nobody can take a cut, freeze it, or lose it. For fans who'd rather use a card, there are subscriptions (next question).

Can listeners subscribe by card instead?

Yes, if you want them to. Connect your own Stripe account in your editor and set a price for a year, six months or three months (we suggest $10, $6 and $3). Subscribers hear all your paid music and aren't charged by the minute. Stripe pays you directly and takes its usual fee, about 3% + 30¢ (plus Stripe Billing's small fee on subscriptions); Amply takes nothing.

How should I choose a price?

Your editor starts you at half a cent a minute: about two cents for a four-minute song, all of it yours. A fan who listens to you for an hour a day pays you about $9 a month. Most artists should start there and leave it.

How do I get paid, and get the money to my bank?

In your editor, open Earnings and tap Make my wallet with Face ID. Fans' payments land there. You own it; Amply can't touch it.

To move it to your bank, open Earnings → Cash out and choose Coinbase (most countries) or Cash App (US). Face ID sends it; they turn it into ordinary money.

Using Phantom or another wallet instead

Any Solana wallet works, such as Phantom or Solflare: copy its address into Where your money goes. Whenever an app asks which network, the answer is Solana; digital dollars sent on another network won't arrive. Don't use an exchange's deposit address as your payout address: exchanges often ignore small deposits. Receive into your own wallet and move money to the exchange when you cash out.

Your key is the money. With Face ID, it's your passkey, which syncs through your Apple or Google account. With a wallet app, it's the recovery words: write them down and keep them safe. Nobody, Amply included, can recover either.

More questions

Why do payments arrive every few songs, not every minute?

Listeners always pay for exactly the minutes they listened. The app keeps a running total on their phone and sends it every song, every five or every ten (you choose), because each transfer has a tiny fee. Nobody holds the money in between, and nobody approves anything: it just happens. Anything smaller is sent within seven days, the next time they open the app.

Why does a listener need a wallet?

It pays you, and it's how your streaming service knows who's listening, so it can stop people who don't pay. There's no account, no password and no email. The listener's app proves which wallet it holds, and your server lets it play for half an hour at a time.

What does data protection ask of me?

If you charge, your streaming service keeps a small record for each listener: their wallet code, how much they've listened, and when they were last here. Never what they played, and never a name or email. Under data protection law (GDPR) that record is yours to look after. Amply holds nothing.

Already done for you: a privacy page written from what your service actually keeps; listeners can see, download or erase their own record from the app; records are deleted a year after someone was last here.

Yours to do: add an email for privacy questions in Settings, answer anyone who writes within a month (Earnings has Export data and Delete on every listener), and keep your Cloudflare account safe with a strong password and two-step sign-in.

This is how the software works, not legal advice.

How does my editor know who has paid me?

Payments are public records, so your editor reads them in your browser when you open Earnings and matches them to your listeners. Nothing is stored. The comparison is rough: if you raised your price, older listening can look underpaid. Treat a red figure as something to look at, not a verdict.

What happens when I block someone?

Your paid tracks stop playing for that wallet within about half an hour; free tracks still play. They could make a new wallet, which will show up in your Earnings like anyone else. If they ask to have their data erased, the block stays. The list lives only on your own service.

Where does the money actually go?

Straight from the listener's wallet to yours, in one transfer, with nothing in between. If you split with collaborators, everyone's share goes in that same transfer, so either everybody is paid or nobody is.

Do I have to pay tax on this?

Money you earn is income wherever you live, and selling music can mean sales tax or VAT once you pass your country's threshold. Amply files nothing for you, because it isn't part of the payment. We can't give tax advice; when the amounts get real, an hour with an accountant is worth it.

Why do I have to rebuild my streaming service?

It runs in your own Cloudflare account, and Amply has no way in. So improvements don't arrive by themselves: your editor offers a rebuild when there's a new version. It takes about a minute and doesn't touch your music, page or price. Rebuild yours now →
