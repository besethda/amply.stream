Your music, on hosting you own.

Amply doesn't host your music — you do. It lives on your own Cloudflare account, in your name, that you log into and control. We just set it up for you.

Three steps, about five minutes

  • Create a free Cloudflare account
  • Switch on R2 storage — this asks for a card
  • Come back here and we'll build everything

About the card

Cloudflare requires one before storage can be switched on. That's their rule, not ours. Nothing will be charged. Their free tier covers roughly a thousand tracks, and streaming to your listeners costs nothing at any volume — bandwidth is free, which is the whole reason this works.

When you sign up, use a personal email address you'll still have in ten years — not a work or school one. Billing and account notices go there, and this account is where your music lives. Turn on two-factor authentication while you're there.

Connect your Cloudflare account

We'll create three things in your account:

  • An R2 bucket — your audio files
  • A KV namespace — your track list
  • A Worker — serves your music to listeners

We're asking for

  • account.read
  • workers-scripts.write
  • workers-kv-storage.write
  • workers-r2.write
  • access-org.write
  • access-idp.write
  • access-app.write
  • access-policy.write

The last four put your editor on your own node behind a sign-in, so adding music never goes through us. They're used once, during setup, and we keep no access afterwards.

We keep no access after this. No password, no stored permission, nothing that lets us back in later. Our access lasts for the few seconds this takes, and then it's gone. If you ever want to change something, you authorise us again.

This names your files and your web address. You can change how it's displayed later, but the address is harder to change — so pick the name you release music under.

Used only to lock your editor to you. Signing in later emails you a code — there's no password to lose, and we never store the address anywhere.

Which account?

You're a member of more than one Cloudflare account. Pick the one your music should live in — this matters, so don't put it in an account that belongs to someone else or to a job.
